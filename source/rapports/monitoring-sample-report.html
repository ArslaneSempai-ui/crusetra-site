04

What this sample cannot tell

This history holds only the alerts your current program raised and your analysts dispositioned: a pattern nobody ever alerted on is invisible to it, and recall here means recall among the cases your analysts confirmed. 1200 of the 1200 cases had no transaction before their window; the scenarios that compare a case with its own past or with its peers score 0 there.

05

How it was measured

Each alert is rebuilt into a case from your transactions, then every scenario is replayed at every threshold. Recall is the share of the cases your analysts confirmed that the scenario would have raised; the false-alert rate is the share of the cases they closed without action that it would have raised. Each rate carries a 95 % interval (Wilson) and its n. Recall is quoted from 5 confirmed cases and a false-alert rate from 20 benign cases. The recommended cell is the one with the fewest false alerts among those whose recall lower bound holds the floor the client declares.

06

What this report does not do

It does not see a value: the sealed record holds counts and rates per scenario and threshold, the first and last decision dates, the declared volume, the scales it was measured under, a disposition and a history flag per alert, and the SHA-256 of the two files. It holds no account, no transaction date, no country, no amount written out and no score per alert. The steps of the thresholds place each alert's largest amount between two values of the declared scale, and its transaction count the same way. The alert ids and the two file names come back in the record; this page prints none of them. It does not test robustness on cases nobody fabricated: that is measured apart, on the public record, which is entirely fabricated and says so. It measures these 1200 alerts; another program or another period needs its own sample.
