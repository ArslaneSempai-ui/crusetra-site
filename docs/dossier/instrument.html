What this rests on, for your IT auditor
  • Our public test set. releve-public.json in the repository, with a content hash (a checksum) of 67ff03042f26708e, measured at commit 34b19c8 on 2026-09-07. The script that builds this page checks that checksum first. It then rebuilds each question’s state from its own answers, in the order the contract sets, and publishes nothing if one line disagrees.
  • The reports come from the suite’s own public test sets. The reports judged here are the public records of Crusetra’s tools (the reader, the matcher that compares names, the scenario, the factor): published, sealed, verifiable by anyone. If a question has none, it is shown as a labeled blank, and no column is guessed.
  • The five controls are applied in a fixed order. present, frozen, signed, fresh, consistent: the state reached is the highest control held without a gap in that order. A held control above a hole counts for nothing, and the table shows why.
  • Freshness is judged against a declared validity period. Each measurement’s age is judged against the validity period written in the dossier itself (90 days), never against an unstated habit.
What this page cannot do
  • Your data. This page cannot read it: no network request leaves it (the browser’s own security rules forbid them), nothing is loaded from elsewhere, and there is no input field to paste a report into.
  • A verdict without its sentence. Each cell opens into the record’s own words: what was checked, on which file, and what the next control still needs.

Assemble the dossier of your own reports

This page shows our own dossier, over our public test sets. Yours is assembled at home, by the tool, from the signed reports the suite’s tools left on your machine, and nothing about them leaves your machine.

$ cd crusetra-dossier
$ npm ci --ignore-scripts
$ npm run dossier -- --reports=a-measured.json,b-measured.json
